Go2 EDU U3 · Bring-Up Checklist

Kepler Sim-to-Real

I trained a velocity-tracking locomotion policy for our Go2 quadruped in NVIDIA Isaac Lab, checked it in a second physics engine (MuJoCo, 7 of 7 flat-ground tests passed), and deployed it on the robot's onboard computer in place of Unitree's stock controller. This is the runbook I wrote for that deployment: every step from a cold robot to the first policy-driven walk.

Tick steps as you go. Recorded values save in your browser only, because half of them are needed three stages later.

A

Get on the wire and get a shell

10 min

Nothing else can start until you have an SSH session. Kepler's addressing is not the same as Vera's, so do not assume the R1 numbers.

  1. ExpectRoughly 30 to 60 seconds. Wait for the fans and LEDs to settle before plugging anything in.

  2. ExpectThe body port, not a backpack. Kepler's compute is internal, so the R1 routing you had to work out for Vera does not apply here.

  3. ipconfig | findstr 192.168.123

    Expect192.168.123.99. If it is missing, the adapter reverted to DHCP and nothing below will find the robot.

  4. powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File C:\robotics\scan_kepler.ps1

    ExpectTwo minutes. Allow the inbound UDP prompt for Private networks or the DDS section finds nothing. It ends by printing the exact ssh line.

  6. ssh unitree@<the IP above>

    ExpectA password prompt.

    If refusedDo not brute force it. Ask the vendor for the account details.

What the scan found, and why it matters later

Four hosts on the wire. The split between them is the single most important thing in this runbook, because it is what makes stage I work the way it does.

192.168.123.18The Jetson. SSH and a Tornado web service. This is where everything you build runs.
192.168.123.161The control board. Publishes RTPS on domain 0, so it owns the motors. Port 80 only, no SSH.
192.168.123.20TTL 255, so embedded. Likely the lidar or a motor MCU. Not used here.
192.168.123.99Your workstation.

DDS discovery was heard on domain 0, which matches the ChannelFactory::Instance()->Init(0, ...) hardcoded in the controller's main.cpp. One less thing to configure.

Probe before you change anything

10 min

Seven answers. Every one of them is used later, and two of them change what you do next. All read-only.

  1. uname -m

    Expectaarch64. If it says x86_64 your life gets easier and you skip stage F entirely.

  2. cat /etc/os-release | head -3
uname -r

    ExpectUbuntu 20.04. The SSH banner already said OpenSSH_8.2p1 Ubuntu-4ubuntu0.5, which is 20.04's build, so this is a confirmation rather than a discovery.

  3. ip -brief address

    ExpectThe one carrying a 192.168.123.x address. Vera's was eth10 with eth0 down, so do not assume eth0.

  4. df -h /

    Under 3 GBStop and clear space first. A build that dies on a full disk leaves a confusing mess.

  5. free -h; nproc

    RuleUse -j4 if RAM is 8 GB or less. C++ with heavy templates will OOM a small board at -j$(nproc).

  6. systemctl list-units --type=service | grep -iE "sport|motion|unitree|robot"

    Read before stage IThe service that owns the motors does not run on this machine. It runs on the control board at 192.168.123.161, which offers no SSH. So systemctl here cannot reach it, and finding nothing is the expected result rather than a problem. Stage I switches it off over DDS instead.

  7. ping -c1 -W2 8.8.8.8
curl -sI https://github.com | head -1

Fork: the robot subnet is isolated

Stages C through F all pull from the internet: apt packages, two git repos, and an onnxruntime tarball. The 192.168.123.x wire is the robot's own control network and normally has no route out.

Easiest path. Put Kepler on the building WiFi for stages C through F, then go back to the isolated wire for stages I and J. From the SSH session:

nmcli device wifi list
sudo nmcli device wifi connect "<SSID>" password "<pw>"
ping -c1 8.8.8.8   # confirm before continuing

If WiFi is not an option, download everything on the workstation and push it over with scp. That works for the two repos and the onnxruntime tarball. It does not work well for apt, so check first whether the packages in stage C are already installed. Many Unitree images ship with most of them.

The lowcmd guard does not stop anything

main.cpp checks whether another process holds the lowcmd channel and logs a critical error telling you to close it first. The exit(0) on that branch is commented out, so it prints the warning and continues anyway.

If sport_mode is still running when you start your controller, two programs will fight over the same twelve motors while the log narrates it. Stage I is a real step, not a formality.

Worth knowing: CPU only is genuinely fine

The policy is a three layer network, roughly 188,000 parameters, evaluated at 50 Hz. That is nothing on a CPU. The stock aarch64 build is entirely sufficient, so there is no CUDA, no TensorRT and no JetPack version matching anywhere in this build. A whole category of problem does not apply.

I

Release the motors

10 min

Everything up to here was reversible. This is the stage that takes Kepler out of his normal operating mode, and the mechanism is not the obvious one.

  1. Flat onlyThe terrain curriculum never promoted past its first level across 23,043 training iterations. In simulation the only falls were forward walks into a 4 cm step. He has no learned competence for thresholds, ramps, cables or rugs.

  3. ip -brief address   # confirm the 192.168.123.x interface is up
  4. cd ~/robotics/go2_service/build
./go2_service <interface from B3> list

    Check the protect columnAnything marked YES will refuse to switch. If the motor service is protected, stop here. That is a vendor question, not something to work around.

  5. ./go2_service <interface from B3> off sport_mode

    Expectret=0 and Switched OFF. Kepler may visibly relax as the control board lets go.

  6. ./go2_service <interface from B3> list

    ExpectThe service now showing off. If it still says on, stop here. The controller will not stop for you.

Why this replaced the systemctl step

The obvious move is sudo systemctl stop sport_mode on the machine you are logged into. It cannot work. That service runs on the control board at 192.168.123.161, which answers on port 80 and nothing else, so there is no shell to run it in.

The SDK's RobotStateClient exists for exactly this, with ServiceList and ServiceSwitch over DDS. The stock SDK example switches sport_mode off, waits five seconds, then switches it back on before exiting, which is the opposite of what you want here. go2_service does one thing per invocation and leaves it that way.

J

First run

take the time it takes
  1. cd ~/robotics/unitree_rl_lab/deploy/robots/go2/build
./go2_ctrl --network <interface from B3>

  2. ExpectWaiting for connection to robot... then Connected to robot. If it hangs on the first line, the interface name is wrong. The DDS domain is already confirmed as 0.

    If you seeThe other process is using the lowcmd channel, kill it immediately. Stage I did not take. The program will carry on regardless if you let it.

  3. ExpectA log line reading Policy directory: ... ending in 2026-09-02_16-04-31. If it names a different folder, stop and check stage G.

  4. LT + APassive to FixStand. Two full seconds of pose interpolation. It has not hung.

  5. Looking forA still, level stance. Buzzing, oscillation or one leg fighting the others means stop and abort now rather than after the policy engages.

  6. startFixStand to Velocity. The policy is now driving.
    LT + BBack to Passive. This is your abort and it works from either state.

    Looking forA stationary, settled stand. In simulation the zero command case held perfectly still at 0.320 m with no drift.

  7. He will undershootTracking runs 60 to 78% of commanded across every direction tested. A 1.0 m/s command produced 0.78 m/s in simulation. Direction is correct, magnitude is low. Do not compensate by commanding harder.

  8. ExpectA controlled slump. Passive is damping only, kd of 3 with no position gain, so it is neither a rigid drop nor a collapse.

You have one automatic safety net

State_RLBase registers a bad_orientation check at 1.0 radian, roughly 57 degrees, which transitions straight to Passive. If Kepler goes over, the controller drops him to damping on its own without waiting for your thumb. This is real and it is already compiled into what you just built.
