Vera Bring-Up

Vera is STEM Innovation Academy's Unitree R1 humanoid. Her compute module arrived uncommissioned and Unitree publishes almost nothing for this model, so I worked out the bring-up from the robot itself and the SDK source. This is the runbook: six ordered steps from a robot on a stand to a scripted arm-and-hand routine. Each step produces the facts the next one needs. Nothing here trains a policy.

01DONE

Reconnaissance

Complete. Kept here because the connection procedure is not obvious and you will need it again.

  1. Ethernet goes into the backpack, not the robot. The R1 body has one RJ45 on its top interface panel, next to the 24V and 36V outputs. That jack reaches the main control board only. The Orin has its own RJ45 on the compute backpack. The backpack ships uncommissioned. Its XT30 power input and its RJ45 were both empty out of the box, which is why the Orin appeared dead. Check power before you debug anything else.
  2. Workstation static at 192.168.123.99, mask 255.255.255.0, no gateway.
  3. Shell in. 
    ssh unitree@192.168.123.164
    Pick 1 for foxy at the ROS prompt. Change the default password on first login.
  4. Bind CycloneDDS to her interface or discovery returns nothing. Her NIC is eth10, which nothing would guess. 
    cat > ~/cdds.xml <<'EOF'
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8" ?>
<CycloneDDS xmlns="https://cdds.io/config">
  <Domain id="any">
    <General>
      <Interfaces>
        <NetworkInterface name="eth10" priority="default" multicast="default" />
      </Interfaces>
      <AllowMulticast>true</AllowMulticast>
    </General>
  </Domain>
</CycloneDDS>
EOF
echo 'export CYCLONEDDS_URI=file:///home/unitree/cdds.xml' >> ~/.bashrc
  5. Verify. 
    ros2 topic list
timeout 15 ddsls dcpspublication

02NOW

Read her state

Prove the link end to end. Still no commands.

  1. Start small. Battery telemetry proves the whole type chain without flooding the terminal. 
    timeout 3 ros2 topic echo /lf/bmsstate
  2. Then joint state. Use the low-frequency topic first; /lowstate runs at full control rate. 
    ros2 topic echo /lf/lowstate
  3. The real test: move a joint by hand. With her powered but damped, move an elbow. The printed number must change. That proves you are reading the robot, not a stale buffer.
  4. Record the joint index map. The official table runs 0 to 30 with EMPTY at 14, 20, 21, 27 and 28. That is 26 real DOF in a 31-slot array. Write down which index moved when you moved the elbow, and confirm it against that table.
Checkpoint Numbers change when you physically move a joint, and you have a written index map. Observability is what makes every later step debuggable.
If ros2 echo cannot resolve the type The Unitree message packages are not in her ROS environment. Fall back to unitree_sdk2_python, which she does not yet have and cannot download. Transfer it from the workstation by scp, or give her a route out first.
03

High-level posture

First movement under our control, balance safeguards left on.

needs step 2

  1. Hoist her properly. Feet clear of the floor, straps taking full weight, nothing within arm's reach. Non-negotiable, for the reason in the gate below.
  2. Physical e-stop in your hand before a single command is sent. Test it while she is in zero torque. Also find out what writes to /lf/emergency_stop.
  3. The client already exists and is compiled. No code to write. 
    cd ~/unitree_sdk2/build/bin
./r1_loco_client --network_interface=eth10 --get_fsm_id
    One command per invocation. The parser loads flags into a std::map, so they execute in alphabetical order, not the order you type. --stand_up --damp runs damp first.
  4. Her state machine, read out of r1_loco_client.hpp:
    idStateFlag
    0Zero torque. Motors limp. She boots into this.--zero_torque
    1Damp. The safe holding state.--damp
    2Squat. Wrapper commented out by Unitree.—
    3Sit. Wrapper commented out by Unitree.—
    4Stand up.--stand_up
    811Start / ready.--start
  5. Damp before anything else, and return to damp between every experiment. It is the state to reach for without looking.
  6. Then start, then stand. Whether 811 must precede 4 is unverified. Try --start first and watch what the FSM id reads back as.
Safety gate · she has no way down Sit and squat are commented out for the R1. Her only descent paths are --damp and --zero_torque, and both drop her weight onto whatever is underneath. There is no graceful sit. She stays in straps for every command in this step, with a second person in the room.
Two traps in the source Continuous move is a ten-day command. Move() with continous_move set passes a duration of 864000 seconds. Left at default, every velocity command expires after 1 second, which is a built-in deadman. Do not turn that off.

Ids 2 and 3 are still reachable via --set_fsm_id=2. A vendor commenting out a call on one model and not another usually means untested. That is a vendor question, not a shortcut.
Service name R1's LocoClient declares LOCO_SERVICE_NAME = "sport", so it talks to rt/api/sport/request, not rt/api/loco/request. Both topics exist on her bus. The client uses sport.
04DEMO

Arm actions

The first demo routine. Twenty-two actions ship on the robot.

needs step 3

  1. Use the arm action service, not raw arm_sdk. Unitree ships no R1 arm_sdk example, and the G1 one is unsafe here for the reason in the trap below. The action client is already compiled and confirmed working against Vera. 
    cd ~/unitree_sdk2/build/bin
./g1_arm_action_example --network eth10 --list
./g1_arm_action_example --network eth10 --id 99
  2. Learn 99 release_arm first. It hands the arms back. It is your software abort and it belongs in a second terminal, ready, before any other id runs.
  3. The safe half of the menu. No person inside arm's reach:
    idActionidAction
    26wave_above_head35emphasize
    25wave_under_head31extend_right_arm_forward
    15both_hands_up33right_hand_on_heart
    23right_hand_up24ultraman_ray
    36forward_push22refuse
    11 12 13blow_kiss (both / left / right)28 29 30box_win variants
    Contact actions, not before the routine is rehearsed cold: 18 high_five, 27 shake_hand, 19 hug, 17 clamp. These put a person inside her working radius by design.
  4. Test every id you intend to use, one at a time. This action set was built for a 7-DOF arm and Vera's are 5-DOF. Some will refuse, some may move oddly. Unitree's own error handler says "if an error is still returned at this point, ignore this action", which tells you they expect failures.
  5. Watch rt/arm/action/state while it runs. It reports what the arm controller thinks it is doing.
  6. Custom actions are supported and yours is empty. The list returns [[builtins], []]. The client's --name and --stop flags drive custom actions, which is the route to a bespoke STEMIA routine.
Safety gate Actions refuse unless she is in an active FSM state, so Step 3 comes first and she is hoisted. Clear a full arm's radius. Have --id 99 queued in a second terminal before the first run. Actions are all under 10 seconds, so nothing you trigger runs away.
Never run g1_arm5_sdk_dds_example against Vera It writes its authority weight into motor_cmd[29], which on the G1 is empty padding. On the R1, index 29 is HEAD_PITCH. The ramp goes to 1.0 radian against a limit of ±0.63. It also commands waist indices 12 and 13, which are transposed relative to Vera, and index 14, which has no motor. Written for a robot with a different joint count.
Checkpoint One chosen action runs three times identically, with release_arm cleanly returning control each time. Repeatability is what makes it demo-safe.
05DONE

The hands

Open and close both hands, then fold them into the arm routine.

needs step 2

  1. Start the hand server. It runs in the foreground and holds the serial ports. Leave that terminal alone. 
    cd ~/brainco_hand_service/bin
sudo ./brainco_hand_server --network eth10
    The README says eth0 because it was written for a G1. Vera's interface is eth10. That one substitution is the difference between working and silently finding nothing.
  2. Expect this. The failed probe is the server trying ports in order until a device answers, not an error. 
    left hand bound to /dev/ttyHAND0 port
right hand bound to /dev/ttyHAND1 port
Starting worker for left (slave 126)
Starting worker for right (slave 127)
  3. Second SSH session, run the test. The hand opens and closes on a loop until you stop it. 
    cd ~/brainco_hand_service/bin
sudo ./test_brainco_hand_server left
# Ctrl+C to stop, then:
sudo ./test_brainco_hand_server right
  4. Then write your own commands. Topics are rt/brainco/{left,right}/{cmd,state}, not the dex3 names. Position and speed are both normalised 0 to 1, and Unitree recommends speed 1.0 on every finger. 
    finger order: [Thumb, Thumb_aux, Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky]
    Six values per hand, which is where the 6 DOF goes: two thumb joints plus four fingers.
Checkpoint · cleared 10 Sep Left hand clenching and opening on command. That proves the whole chain: workstation, backpack ethernet, Orin, CycloneDDS on eth10, CH343 serial bridge, BrainCo motor. Nothing in that path is theoretical now.
Do not run hand_set.sh It reads like setup and is actually a teardown: it deletes ~/brainco_hand_service and removes the autostart unit. A backup is at ~/brainco_hand_service.bak.
Loose end Something publishes rt/dex3/{left,right}/state even with the BrainCo server stopped, and it does not hold the serial ports. Worth identifying before the hands go into the arm routine, so two things are not claiming them during a demo.
06DONE

Camera frames

Get frames off the head. Foundation for vision work later.

  1. Both cameras are plain V4L2 devices. The binocular module is two independent USB cameras, four video nodes. Exact parameters, taken from Unitree's own commissioning history on this robot: 
    ffplay -f v4l2 -input_format mjpeg -video_size 2688x1520 -i /dev/video0
ffplay -f v4l2 -input_format mjpeg -video_size 2688x1520 -i /dev/video2
  2. Grab one frame and write it to disk. That is the whole goal. A file you can open. OpenCV with the same MJPEG and size settings.
  3. The DDS route exists too via rt/api/videohub/request and rt/frontvideostream. Use it when you want frames off-robot rather than on it.
Checkpoint · cleared 10 Sep Frame captured from /dev/video0 at 2688×1520, three channels, written to disk. Everything downstream is ordinary computer vision on an Orin, with nothing Unitree-specific about it. Two eyes a known baseline apart is also the raw material for stereo depth.

Why Vera stays on an isolated wire

rt/api/bashrunner/request is a service that executes shell commands, reachable by anything that can put a packet on this subnet, with no authentication in the DDS layer. That is normal for a robot development bus and it is not a defect to report. It is the reason Vera never touches the school network, and it belongs in the lab's safety documentation alongside the physical hazards.

What this runbook deliberately leaves out

CapabilityWhy not
RL training The R1 is absent from unitree_rl_lab. Its only RL path is unitree_rl_mjlab, which is MuJoCo rather than Isaac Lab. A humanoid policy is a multi-week project even with good tooling.
XR teleoperation xr_teleoperate lists configs only for the upper-body R1 series (R1_A5, R1_A7). Vera is the full-size legged R1, which it does not support.
Voice and language Downgraded from "not doing" to "not yet". Recon found rt/api/voice/request, rt/api/gpt/request, rt/gpt_cmd, rt/gpt_state and rt/audio_msg all live on her bus. The machinery is there and undocumented. Worth an hour once steps 2 to 5 are solid, and worth a vendor question either way.
Walking Standing on a hoist is the honest limit of a bring-up. Free walking is a separate project with real risk.

Kepler is the counterweight here. A converged Go2 velocity policy trained on our own hardware and deployed to the robot is the learning result. See Kepler Sim-to-Real. Vera's contribution is a clean scripted routine and her presence in the same simulation.

Open questions for the vendor, in priority order

  • Where are the R1 Isaac Sim assets? The invoice states the robot "supports simulation environments such as Isaac SIM." There is no R1 USD in unitree_model, no R1 task in unitree_rl_lab, and no R1 in unitree_sim_isaaclab.
  • Is R1 support planned for unitree_rl_lab, and when? The answer decides whether to migrate or commit to unitree_rl_mjlab.
  • What is the supported teleoperation path for the R1? Given it is absent from xr_teleoperate.
  • The wrist-to-hand mounting transform. Exact offset and rotation from wrist_roll_link to the hand flange. A number Unitree certainly has, and one we currently approximate.
  • What is the request schema for rt/api/gpt/request and rt/api/voice/request? Both services are live on the robot and neither appears in any documentation. Ask for the API IDs and payload format.
  • Confirm rt/arm_sdk semantics. We are relying on it commanding the arms while the balance controller keeps the legs. Get that confirmed rather than assumed, along with the weight or enable field it expects.
  • How is the compute backpack meant to be commissioned? Ours arrived with power and ethernet unconnected and no printed instruction. Which rail feeds it, 24V or 36V, and does anything else need connecting.
  • When does unitree_rl_lab move to Isaac Lab 3.0 and Newton? The main branch is frozen; 3.0 is still beta.

Standing rules

  • Hoist before command. Every session, not just the first.
  • E-stop in reach before anything is sent.
  • Damp is home. Make it the easiest thing to trigger.
  • Simulate first. Any pose gets checked in vera_live.py before it reaches hardware.
  • Never publish low-level commands while the high-level controller is running. Two controllers fighting over the same joints is how a robot gets damaged.
  • Prompt check. (env_isaaclab) at the front of the line, or the Isaac Lab commands will fail.
